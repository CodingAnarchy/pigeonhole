### Added
- **`phdb serve`: `Feed.Subscribe`** (D313, ICR 0112, #1397): the change feed as a gRPC server stream, or over HTTP as NDJSON or server-sent events (each event's `id:` is its token, so `Last-Event-ID` resumes).
  - **Shared reads:** streams on the same table set share one engine subscription, a hub. The shard threads wake one reader for it, and the WAL is read once, whatever the number of subscribers. A hub keeps its recent commits in a ring, about 380 bytes a commit with 100-byte values, within its share of `--feed-ring-bytes`.
  - **Catching up:** a stream that starts from a token, a snapshot or the oldest commit, or falls off its hub's ring, reads on its own until it catches up. Its events and tokens are the same either way.
  - **Row prefixes:** a stream with a row `prefix` gets the changes to matching rows only. It reads which rows a commit touches straight from the hub's shared event, so prefix subscribers keep up like unfiltered ones.
  - **Delivery:** each stream buffers up to 64 events (256 KiB at most) its client hasn't taken, so events that are ready go out together. Quiet streams send progress events and keepalives. A client connection carries at most 128 streams: open another connection for more.
  - **Threads:** delivery runs on threads of its own, `--feed-threads` (default: a quarter of the CPUs the shards don't use, at least 1), so a put never waits behind feed streams. Hubs read the engine on `--feed-hub-threads` (default: a quarter of `--feed-threads`, at least 1). Raise it on a feed-heavy server with CPUs to spare: with one such thread, 16 busy subscriptions can fall seconds behind, while each thread past the default costs puts 13–38% at p50. A subscription with a single stream runs on the feed threads beside it, saving a handoff on every commit, and moves to the hub threads when a second stream joins.
  - **Flags:** `--max-streams` (256), `--max-feed-hubs` (32), `--max-catch-up` (16), `--feed-ring-bytes` (256 MiB in all), `--feed-threads`, `--feed-hub-threads`, and `--http2-send-buffer`, what one HTTP/2 stream may queue for its client (default: hyper's 400 KiB). The guide recommends HTTP/2 adaptive flow control for feed clients: it took 16 subscribers' delivery lag from about 18 ms to 0.5 ms.
  - **Diagnosis:** on SIGUSR1 (Unix), the server writes a report of its hubs to stderr: how far each hub's engine feed is behind the database, its ring against its share of `--feed-ring-bytes`, and where its streams are (on the ring and how far behind its head, on their own catch-up reads, or waiting for a `--max-catch-up` slot). `/metrics` adds `pigeonhole_serve_feed_hub_threads`, `pigeonhole_serve_feed_solo_hubs` and `pigeonhole_serve_feed_hub_moves_total`.
